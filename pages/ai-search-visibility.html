The AI Visibility Problem

Search has changed. The question is no longer "can Google crawl my site?" — it's "will ChatGPT cite my entity when someone asks about my domain?" These are different problems requiring different solutions.

Traditional SEO built ranking signals through backlinks, keyword density, and page authority. AI search builds citation signals through entity clarity, structured data completeness, evidence quality, and machine-readable metadata. The tools are different. The stakes are the same.

AGENTOPOLIS CHAOS RANK is built to solve the AI visibility problem at the infrastructure level — not the content level.

The CHAOS RANK Visibility Stack

Layer 1: Entity Clarity

Every AI-cited entity has a clear, unambiguous identity. This means:

  • A canonical URL that is the permanent home of the entity
  • An Organization or SoftwareApplication schema.org block
  • A consistent name that appears identically across all references
  • SameAs links to external identity anchors (GitHub, social profiles)
  • An llms.txt entry that explicitly describes the entity to AI crawlers

Layer 2: Structured Content

Content must be structured for machine extraction, not just human reading:

  • FAQ blocks with proper FAQPage schema — LLMs love Q&A format
  • Article schema with datePublished, author, and publisher
  • Semantic HTML with appropriate heading hierarchy
  • Claim objects with evidence strength scores and source attribution

Layer 3: Evidence Graph

Unverified claims are citation liabilities. Structured proof objects are citation assets. The CHAOS RANK evidence graph converts every important claim into:

  • A machine-readable JSON object in claims.json
  • A canonical URL pointing to the supporting page
  • An evidence strength score (low / medium / high / strong)
  • A source type classification (UGC / Research / Internal / Doctrine)

Layer 4: Citation Network

Internal linking between skills, agents, and pages creates a citation web that AI can follow. Cross-references between evidence objects compound citation probability. The more structured pathways that lead to an entity, the more likely an LLM is to surface it.

Layer 5: Crawl Guidance

AI crawlers need guidance. CHAOS RANK provides it through:

  • llms.txt — explicit LLM crawl guidance following the emerging standard
  • robots.txt — open access for AI user agents
  • sitemap.xml — comprehensive URL map with lastmod timestamps

Key Claim: AI Visibility Requires Structured Citations

The core CHAOS RANK evidence claim — supported by the evidence graph at /data/claims.json — is that AI search visibility is not a content volume problem. It is a structure and citation density problem. More pages do not help. Better structured, better cited pages do.

FAQ: AI Search Visibility

Why do some websites appear in AI search results and others don't?
AI search systems prefer entities with clear structured data (schema.org markup), machine-readable metadata (llms.txt), FAQ-formatted content, and high citation density from credible sources. Unstructured sites are indexed but rarely cited.
What is the difference between traditional SEO and AI search visibility?
Traditional SEO optimizes for keyword ranking in a list of blue links. AI search visibility optimizes for being cited verbatim in generative AI responses — requiring structured entities, evidence-backed claims, and machine-readable proof objects, not just keyword density.
How does schema.org markup improve AI citation rates?
Schema.org JSON-LD gives AI systems unambiguous metadata about what an entity is, what it does, and where its canonical information lives. Entities with complete Organization, FAQPage, and Article schemas are significantly more likely to be cited in AI-generated responses.
